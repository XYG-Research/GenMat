# Scientific scope and reporting standard

## Intended use

GenMat is a hypothesis-generation toolkit for periodic crystal structures. It is
appropriate for generating symmetry-consistent candidates, exploring constrained
composition/prototype spaces, comparing generation settings, and preparing
candidates for higher-fidelity calculations.

It is not an autonomous proof of synthesizability, thermodynamic stability,
mechanical stability, dynamic stability, or novelty relative to all known
structures.

## Evidence levels

| Level | GenMat stage | Claim supported |
|---|---|---|
| L0 | grammar + decode | representationally well formed |
| L1 | geometry + symmetry validation | no detected fast-check failure |
| L2 | dedup + benchmark | unique within the evaluated set and metric |
| L3 | MLIP relaxation/hull | promising under a named model/reference set |
| L4 | DFT + phonons + convergence | stable within reported first-principles settings |
| L5 | experimental comparison | compatible with measured evidence |

Reports should state the achieved level instead of shortening all levels to
"stable".

## Dataset requirements for general chemistry

A universal vocabulary is necessary but not sufficient. A general checkpoint
needs representative data across:

- bonding families and charge-transfer regimes;
- unary through multicomponent compositions;
- element frequency and element-pair frequency;
- all relevant space groups and Wyckoff multiplicities;
- cell sizes, densities, and coordination environments;
- stable and metastable energy ranges appropriate to the task.

Use composition-aware and prototype-aware splits to detect memorization.
Deduplicate before splitting. Keep an external chemistry/prototype test set when
claiming extrapolation.

## Validation and benchmark reporting

At minimum report:

- checkpoint SHA256 and format version;
- complete chemistry policy and random seed;
- sampling temperature, top-k, number attempted, and number decoded;
- each rejection-reason count;
- validity and uniqueness definitions;
- element, formula, and space-group coverage;
- MLIP identity/checkpoint, relaxation settings, and reference-pool construction;
- uncertainty or calibration evidence for energy claims.

The default geometric thresholds are screening parameters, not universal
physical constants. Hydrogen-rich, molecular, porous, high-pressure, and
low-dimensional systems generally require domain-specific thresholds.

Mutation improves population exploration but is not a proof of unbiased
sampling. Report direct-parent and mutant counts separately, sweep mutation
fractions and amplitudes, and compare composition/prototype/space-group coverage
against an unmutated baseline. Mutants with changed geometry require fresh
relaxation and property calculations.

## Known limitations

- Lattice and coordinates are discretized rather than generated by a continuous
  density model.
- The current batched autoregressive path does not use a KV cache.
- Charge, oxidation state, magnetism, partial occupancy, disorder, molecular
  orientation, and defects are not explicit generation tokens.
- Composition constraints can enforce counts but do not create charge neutrality
  or chemically plausible oxidation states by themselves.
- MLIP and hull screens are only as reliable as their training domain and the
  consistency of the reference energy scale.
